Sites

CSV columns: name, lat, lon, period, type, notes, depth. period can be a period id (e.g. eneo), a name, or years like -3800..-3400. A numeric depth (m, top of cultural layer) also calibrates the depth model.

      My added maps

      Add online map

      Paste a tile or WMS address from a historical map service you have access to (for example Mapire / Arcanum Habsburg surveys, national library or archive map servers). Check each service's terms of use.

      Georeference a scan

      Click a point on the scan, then the same point on the map (a church, road junction or river bend). Add at least 3 pairs, spread around the sheet; more is better. The fit is affine, which is fine for one map sheet.

      Digitise features from maps

      Click the map to record a feature you see on an old map (barrow symbol, vanished village, mill, church, earthwork). It becomes a site with the map's year.

      Survey planning

        Promising terrain (voids)

        Land as far as possible from settlements: the gaps midway between them score highest. Zoom to an area (zoom ≥ 9), load settlements, then compute.

        Shape recognition in imagery

        Scans satellite imagery around the best voids for circles (barrows, ring ditches, house rings; 8–40 m radius) and rectangles (enclosures, foundations; 15–150 m). Detections raise the promise score around them. Heuristic: review each one.

            Desk-based assessment

            Draw the project area: click to add vertices, double-click to finish. The report lists known sites within the buffer, predicted potential, landforms and expected depths.

            Point inspector

            Click anywhere on the map: terrain, site potential, expected depth per period, and nearby sites.

            Field log

            Record finds and depth observations (stored only in this browser). Observations with a depth and period calibrate the depth model within ~20 km.
